Implementation view

Designed as one ERP. Introduced in controlled stages.

Core principle

The first release must complete the full operating chain. It should not create isolated departmental systems.

  • 01
    Financial foundation

    Accounts, budgets, costs, payments and reporting.

  • 02
    Supply Chain

    Procurement, suppliers, stores, inventory and core logistics.

  • 03
    Manufacturing

    Planning, materials, WIP, output, waste, rework and costing.

  • 04
    Quality

    Incoming, in-process and finished-product control.

  • 05
    Sales & Distribution

    Orders, finished-goods allocation, delivery, invoice and collection.

  • 06
    Enterprise controls

    Users, approvals, audit, master data and dashboards.

Expansion principle

Later modules will plug into the same financial, material, workflow and reporting foundation.

  • Plant & machinery maintenance
  • Advanced fleet and GPS
  • Civil works and projects
  • HR and payroll
  • Supplier and customer portals
  • Advanced warehouse automation
  • Machine and IoT integration
  • Advanced planning and MES
  • Business intelligence and AI

What management gains

Control that follows the work—not a report assembled afterwards.

One source of truth

No repeated entry across departments.

Accurate product cost

Material, WIP, waste and output remain accounted for.

Visible accountability

Every approval, change and exception has an owner.

Management visibility

Live operational, financial and exception reporting.

Recommended next step

Confirm the structure. Then map the company's exact processes.

Focused workshops with Finance, Supply Chain, Production, Quality, Sales and Management will define the real approval levels, reports, production stages and costing rules before technical design begins.
